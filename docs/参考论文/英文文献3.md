Abstract
RFM analysis is the behavior-based model that classifies customers based on the recency (R), frequency (F), and monetary (M) of their transactions. A common way to use RFM analysis is to aggregate all data to generate R, F, and M values rather than considering goods or services. However, the RFM values should be calculated by considering the transaction characteristics of the Rooms, Food and Beverage, and Banquet divisions of the hotel. Therefore, this study proposes two-stage clustering with RFM analysis to profile profitable customers. The purpose of first and second-stage clustering is to find active and profitable customers and analyze their transaction characteristics, respectively. The customer data used in this study was collected from a hotel chain in South Korea. The study found that hotel customers have specific divisions they are interested in and that they can be classified based on their transaction activity.
Introduction
The hotel industry has tried many ways to build relationships with customers. Inspired by the success of airlines, loyalty programs were developed to reward returning customers (Sunny Hu et al., 2010). Loyalty programs are a way to increase the loyalty of your hotel guests (Hwang et al., 2019), and have proven to be a powerful way to collect rich customer data (Kopalle et al., 2012, Venkatesan and Farris, 2012). When customers join a hotel loyalty program, they are asked to fill out the demographic data such as name, and email address to receive benefits like rewards points. Through the loyalty program, customer transaction data are tracked and collected when they purchase services or products at the hotel (Magnini et al., 2003).
The hotel industry is becoming increasingly competitive, so customized services and Customer Relations Management (CRM) play a pivotal role (Padilla-Meléndez and Garrido-Moreno, 2014). Hotels need to understand their customers' needs to seize new business opportunities (Wu and Li, 2011). This requires the effective use of ICT in business operations to implement a successful CRM (Sigala, 2005). Throughout the COVID-19 pandemic, the importance of CRM in the hotel industry has grown even more. (Afaq et al., 2023, Chi, 2021). Therefore, hotels need to use data to provide targeted and personalized services and products to their guests. To provide a product or service that is personalized for each customer, the hotels need to understand the customers’ characteristics by analyzing the loyalty program data such as demographics, and transactions. Data mining techniques such as RFM and cluster analysis can be used to analyze these data. RFM and cluster analysis uses customer data to classify customers based on the recency, frequency, and monetary of their transactions and has been used in various industries like apparel (Cheng and Chen, 2009).
A common approach for RFM analysis is to integrate customer transaction data into a single criterion and use the quintile method. RFM analysis that integrates into a single criterion does not consider the services or products customers have purchased. It means that all customer's transaction data is integrated to calculate the recency, frequency, and monetary value. The quintile method splits all customers into 125 clusters by dividing the values of R, F, and M into five dimensions of 20 % each (Hosseini et al., 2010). However, previous studies that have classified customers using RFM analysis in this common way have the following limitations. Firstly, the integration of customer transaction data into a single criterion does not properly consider the nature of the service or product that customers have purchased. There are very few studies using the common method of RFM analysis in the hotel industry, and even the few that can be found do not distinguish between the Rooms, Food & Beverage (F&B), and Banquet customers (Dursun and Caber, 2016, Dursun-Cengizci and Caber, 2024). But, for example, it is hard to imagine that transactions in the Rooms and F&B divisions have similar characteristics. Each division has different transaction patterns, such as frequency of visits and total spending. If customers are classified by integrating all of their transactions into a single criterion that does not consider the characteristics of the hotel, it will be difficult to analyze their transaction patterns properly. However, previous studies that integrate customer data into a single criterion do not properly consider the characteristics of the services or products of hotels. Secondly, it would be difficult to manage every cluster generated by the quintile method for hotels. If a hotel tries to manage 125 clusters, they need a lot of time to analyze the customer characteristics such as demographics, and transaction patterns. This means that the quintile method is not suitable for application in the hotel industry. The customer classification technique is needed to determine how many clusters to divide customers into if the quintile method is not used. It should also be able to profile customers who use the Rooms, F&B, and Banquet divisions, which have different transaction patterns. To fill this research gap, this study aims to propose a suitable customer classification technique to classify hotel customers using multiple divisions with different service characteristics.
Therefore, this study proposes the two-stage RFM and clustering analysis to understand the characteristics of the hotel division. RFM values are calculated for each of the following divisions: the Rooms, F&B, and Banquet. Based on these values, the customers are classified into two-stages, and each stage has a different purpose. This study used the customer data of hotel chains in South Korea. The data is 5 years (2015 ∼ 2019) of transaction data from 8 hotels in Seoul and Incheon. The purpose of the first stage is to find active and profitable customers among them. The purpose of the second stage is to analyze their characteristics such as transaction patterns. This study uses customer data from eight 3–5 star hotels of a hotel chain located in Seoul and Incheon, South Korea, two urban centers. For 3–5 star hotels located in urban centers, rather than resort destinations, the F&B and Banquet divisions are likely to be dominated by domestic customers, although there may be many foreign customers in the Rooms division. While the Rooms customers are more likely to be used by non-residents of the downtown and nearby areas, the F&B and Banquet divisions are more likely to be used by residents of the downtown and nearby areas. This is not unique to Korea, but rather a common feature of 3–5 star hotels downtown. Therefore, the results of this study may be generally applicable to 3–5 star hotels located downtown rather than resort destinations.
The model in this study can help identify customer characteristics by classifying them based on their transaction history data. This can contribute to providing personalized products or services to customers. In addition, it can help hotels improve their operational efficiency and profitability by properly classifying their customers, which can lead to customized marketing strategies.
